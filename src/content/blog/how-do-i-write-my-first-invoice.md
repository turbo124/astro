## What Should Freelance Invoices Include?

Invoices are standard elements of business paperwork, but new freelancers or those who haven’t written their own invoices before often find it difficult to know where to start. Here’s a quick summary of the essential requirements:

- Invoice date and payment due date: Clients need to have an invoice date to enter your invoice into their bookkeeping systems and schedule a payment.

- The word ‘invoice’: Documents that don’t obviously state that they are invoices can be missed, overlooked, or considered invalid.

- A breakdown of each item: Itemizing invoices is best practice because it reflects transparency and ensures clients can check off each charge to verify they are consistent with the work commissioned.

- Total payable: You need to include applicable tax charges, depending on where you live, and present a bold final figure showing the balance.

- Both party details: An invoice should state who it is from and your contact details and reflect the payee's name, address, and communication information.

- Payment information: A client needs to know where to send the remittance, so you can either add your bank or payment transfer details at the bottom or insert a payment link for efficiency.

The next step is to consider formatting because even though an invoice might seem straightforward, all these details should be set out properly so it is easy to scan the document and approve it for payment.
