Invoices are sent whenever someone makes a business transaction that involves the delivery of a product or service. Invoices are a fundamental aspect of running any business.

Invoices are a formal document that provides the information required to get paid by customers and aid in establishing a person or business as a professional in their industry. Additionally, these invoices help businesses and individuals stay organized during tax filings. 

An invoice is an itemized commercial document used to list the products and services delivered to a customer. They feature the total amount due and the preferred payment method. ## Invoices and Why They Matter

The structure of an invoice can have a large impact in regards to the ability of a business or individual to collect from customers. For example, the exact date that the payment is due should be stated instead of simply saying that the client needs to pay in the next thirty days. 

## Information That Invoices Need to Feature

An invoice is a business document that itemizes and even records a transaction that occurs between a buyer and a seller. The business needs to send an invoice to the customer at the point in time when they deliver a product or complete a specific service.

Some of the most fundamental information that each invoice needs to include are the buyer's business contact information and the vendor's business contact information; it must also feature the delivery date and the total amount owed. 

The invoice needs to clearly state the invoice date, description of the goods or services, and the price of each item. It also needs to include shipping and handling charges, if applicable. It should also include an invoice number and any sales tax owed. 

## The Invoice Number and Its Role

One of the most fundamental aspects of the invoice is the invoice number.

Invoices are typically assigned a sequential number. All invoices must feature an invoice number as a direct part of the invoicing process. Otherwise, customers will not be able to have a trail of information that links the charges to the service or product for their accounting records and payment processes.

Users can also utilize chronological order numbers for their invoices. Sometimes, invoices include a customer identification or project identification number. Invoices are usually not due immediately, so establishing payment terms upfront is also essential.

The invoice date is another critical part of the information on an invoice, and it differs from the invoice number. The invoice date indicates when a company records a transaction and generages a bill for the customer. This date is also related to the payment due date. 

A majority of invoices are based on a thirty-day schedule. Some can be even longer, depending on a company's specific cash flow requirements or its agreement with a customer.

With that in mind, the invoice number needs to be clearly stated on the invoice. Without it, an invoice is not considered a legal document. Because invoice numbers are a mandatory invoice field, they need to be assigned in a manner that makes sense to everyone.

## How Businesses Assign Invoice Numbers

Most businesses will use specific invoice numbers, including the month and year it was issued.

If an invoice was issued in 2023, for example, it could be numbered 230001. As a result, the following invoices would be numbered 230002 and 230003. At the start of each year, the first two numbers would reset to 24, 25, and so on as the year passed. This system can be advantageous as it lets anyone quickly see what year an invoice was issued while following a sequential numbering order.

Businesses and individuals need to know why invoices generally include an invoice number if they want to keep track of their finances and grow their businesses. Invoice numbers are unique, sequential codes that are systematically assigned and are one of the most important aspects of invoicing.
