## How Is a Purchase Invoice Used?

A purchase invoice, sometimes known as a supplier invoice, provides a list of goods or services that have been purchased from a seller. These invoices typically include: the product or service name, quantity, unit price, total cost of the transaction, due date for payment, and contact information for both the buyer and seller. 

Purchase invoices can be used by companies as proof of purchase in order to claim deductions on their taxes. For an auto parts business, these will likely be the invoices you most frequently give to clients. These invoices are ideal for selling parts or installation services as they provide a detailed breakdown of labor and parts costs.

## Why Should You Use A Purchase Invoice?

We recommend using purchase invoices whenever parts are sold or labor is charged to a customer. When you provide clients with a purchase invoice for the services you provide, your expenses and stock are accounted for accurately. This not only helps you stay organized with your product stock, but also serves as documentation of proof of services or goods rendered.

The purchase invoice serves as an important accounting record for both the buyer and the seller. Vendors provide clients with invoices so that the client can track their expenses and manage their accounts payable, and auto parts suppliers have records of sales and labor hours (if applicable). Furthermore, should payment be late or incomplete, you will have an indisputable record of outstanding payments. 

There are some instances [when you would use progress invoicing](https://invoiceninja.com/when-would-you-use-progress-invoicing/) instead. This is an invoicing system that works by creating invoices at regular intervals during a larger scale project, by emitting separate purchase invoices at different times of purchase.

## In Summary

A purchase invoice provides an easy way to track all transactions made by small businesses or freelancers while providing protection against potential charge disputes. When used correctly and consistently, they serve as an important record of your business transactions.
