## What Is an Invoice?

Also known as a bill or tab, an invoice is a document sent by a seller to a buyer with a sum of the amount owed. This document is sent after the goods or services have been delivered to the buyer. It indicates the goods or services delivered, their quantities, and prices agreed upon. The buyer is expected to make payments once they receive the invoice.

Your invoice should clearly state the terms of payment, including the specific date that the amount should be paid. Sometimes you can send an invoice after the buyer has already paid the amount. In such cases, you should clearly indicate on your invoice whether the amount has been paid to avoid confusion.

## How to Create an Invoice without a Company?

As a freelancer or sole proprietor, you may find it difficult to create and send invoices to clients, especially large enterprises, that are accustomed to doing business with established companies. The main reason why this type of invoicing is complex is because your business is unincorporated and therefore you’ll receive all the proceeds and be responsible for your business losses, obligations, and debts.

For instance, if you’re a lawyer offering legal services to businesses and organizations as an individual, you’re allowed to create a personal invoice and send it to your clients as required. Fortunately, you can find a professional invoice template for your invoices. Furthermore, most of these templates are free.

However, if you want a template with advanced features, you’ll be required to pay a small fee. These invoice templates offer numerous benefits, including saving time, creating an element of consistency, and improving accuracy.

Once you’ve chosen the right invoice template, the next step is to fill in the important details. Here are the important details you need to include in your personal invoice:

### Header

This section is very important as it carries critical details, including the name of your business, your contact details (mailing address, email address, website, phone number, etc.), and your logo. Make sure the header looks professional and choose the right font size to make it legible.

### Client Details

Below the header, write your client’s contact details, including their full names, mailing address, physical address, phone number, etc. Normally, this information should be on the opposite side of your contact details to avoid confusion.

### Invoice Details

The details of your invoice should come below your contact information and across the client’s contact details. Invoice details include the invoice number, the date it was prepared, and the due date. When numbering your invoice, use a sequence to make tracking easy.

Also, specify your preferred method of payment, such as cash, credit card, digital payment, or check. In your terms of payment, make it clear if you charge any fees for late payment. This prevents misunderstandings should your clients delay timely payment.

It also offers incentives for your clients to clear their invoices promptly. If you are required to declare taxes after delivering services and receiving payments, you may have to include a tax number in your invoice.
