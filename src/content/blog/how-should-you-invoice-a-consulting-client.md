## Best Practice When Raising Invoices for Consultation Work

If you work in a team, having one person responsible for clerical work and invoicing is advisable to avoid duplications and potentially doubling up on workflows. However, you will likely need to manage your invoices yourself if you are a freelance consultant.

Tips to streamline your invoicing system and billing processes include:

- Making sure fee structures, amounts payable, and the expected total cost of the consultation work are clear from day one: If you have a terms of sale document, this can be a useful way to avoid issues later.

- Keeping accurate records of time spent on each element of a consultation to ensure you aren't losing out by forgetting to invoice billable hours, including travel, where appropriate

- Creating agreements before work begins stating which expenses your client is expected to cover and which are included within your consulting rates–examples such as research, reporting, and travel time could all constitute billable expenses

- Raising an invoice without delay, ensuring your work is still fresh in your client's mind; they can verify the hours or weeks worked and approve your invoice promptly

Payment terms vary between consultants and may depend on the consultation services you offer, but asking for a deposit upfront can be a good way to protect your business from non-payment or slow payers.

Deposits can start from 10% of the project total, extending to 50% in advance, giving you the assurance that your client is committed to the consultation. However, you must keep accurate invoicing records and make sure you subtract the deposit paid from your final payable invoice.
