Invoices and contracts are essential documents that help establish the terms and conditions of a transaction. And just like you may wonder [if an invoice is a receipt](https://invoiceninja.com/is-an-invoice-a-receipt/), you may have asked yourself if an invoice is a contract. While they may seem similar at first glance, there are significant differences between these two documents.

In this post, we’ll explore the nature of invoices and contracts to help you protect your interests. 

## What Is an Invoice?

An invoice is a document outlining the details of a sale or provided service. It typically includes information such as the transaction date, the product or service sold, the quantity, and the price. Why are invoices so important? Well, there are two reasons:

### A Means of Record-Keeping

Invoices document the sale of goods or services and provide a clear record of what was sold, the date it was sold, and for how much. This information helps you keep track of your finances and ensures you’re paying the right amount of taxes.

### A Tool for Getting Paid on Time

By including payment terms on the invoice, you can set clear expectations for your customers regarding payment deadlines, methods of payment, and any late payment penalties.

## What Is a Contract?

A contract is a legally binding agreement between two or more parties that creates an obligation to perform certain actions or deliver specific services. It typically includes provisions specifying the rights and responsibilities of each party involved.

To ensure all parties understand the agreement, a contract typically includes the following key elements:

- Offer—this is the initial proposal that sets out the terms of the agreement and can be made in writing, verbally, or by conduct. Once an offer is made, the other party has the opportunity to accept or reject it. 

- Consideration—this is something of value each party agrees to provide in exchange for the other's promises, such as money, goods, services, or anything else the parties agree upon.

- Acceptance—this is when the contract must be mutually agreed upon or accepted. This means that both parties must agree to the contract terms and be willing to be bound by them.

- Legal capacity—this outlines that each party involved in the agreement must be legally competent, meaning they are fully aware of the terms and obligations of the deal, in order to enter into the contract. 

- Lawful purpose—these are contractual terms that do not violate any laws or public policies. If a contract is found to be illegal, it won’t be enforced.

## Is a Signed Invoice Legally Binding?

Yes, when an invoice is signed by both the buyer and the seller, it becomes a legally binding document. This means both parties have agreed to the terms outlined in the invoice, and they are obligated to fulfill their end of the deal.

Signed invoices are crucial for business owners. If you're providing a service to a client, and the invoice is signed by both you and your client, you now have a legal document proving they’ve agreed to pay you for your services. And if they fail to do so, you have legal recourse to get your due amount.

## The Takeaway

An invoice documents the details of a sale or service and helps you keep track of your finances and get paid on time, while a contract establishes the terms and obligations of a transaction before it happens, making sure all parties involved are on the same page.
