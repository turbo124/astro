To be valid and payable, an auto repair invoice must include certain details, such as a unique invoice number and date of billing, a description of the parts and labor provided (including date of completion), the final amounts payable, payment terms and instructions, and contact information, among other details.

## Why Do I Need to Make an Auto Repair Invoice?

Any auto repair business owner or service provider needs to create invoices to keep their business legitimate and professional. It can be used for various related tasks, including:

- Auto repair

- Oil, brake, tire, or window replacement

- Accident repair

- Fuel system and engine repair

- Air conditioning repair or installation

- Exhaust or transmission repair or testing

- Auto body or auto glass repair

- Bodywork or paintwork repair

An auto repair invoice documents the work that you’ve provided clients and the products you’ve offered them, putting a price on the project so that you are rightly paid for your services. Having an accurate and organized invoice ensures that there are no errors in your payments and lessens the risk of any billing delays.

## How to Invoice an Auto Repair

If you’re wondering [how to invoice an auto repair](https://invoiceninja.com/how-do-i-invoice-an-auto-repair/), here’s a look into the process. Invoicing actually has pre-steps—it starts even before you start working. First, you must settle a price or contract with your client that estimates the cost of the repairs to be done, including the expenses that may be incurred with the exchange or addition of components, or the price of the project overall. 

Once you’ve agreed to the terms, you start providing your services as discussed. When you’re done, you then do the invoice, which starts by detailing the work that you’ve done, referencing this to the prices in your initial estimates. Note that these might differ, granting that there may be unexpected changes during the job. After listing everything down, you just have to calculate the prices and bill this to your customer.

## What to Include in an Auto Repair Invoice

Invoicing for an auto repair is pretty straightforward, but you must make sure that your documents are detailed and error-free so that you are paid what you’re due. Here are the important items that have to be on an auto repair invoice:

### Business Details

Put your logo and the registered name and address of the auto repair company. Include relevant business IDs, as well, especially if it’s required by your state.

### Invoice Number

Each invoice should have its own unique number that appears clearly on the document. This will help you keep your transactions organized as it differentiates each bill from previous ones.

### Date of Issue

The date of issue is the date when the invoice was created and sent to the customer. You must include this as this often ties into your payment terms, like payment deadlines.

### Customer Details

Your client’s details need to be included in the invoice for reference in their own bookkeeping records. This should include their verified name, address, and contact details.

### Item List

The item list details all services and components that you’re billing your client for. This includes all the labor that you’ve put in for the project, as well as any parts or components that you’ve exchanged or installed. Total this amount and place the subtotal at the end.

### Taxes and Other Fees

If there are taxes (such as VAT) or other fees that need to be applied to the bill, make sure to put them.

### Total Amount Payable

The total amount payable is the calculation of your items list (i.e. the subtotal), plus taxes and fees, and minus any discounts. It’s the final charge of the invoice that your customer must pay you.

### Payment Instructions

Provide instructions on how you accept payments, like whether you can only receive cash or if you have alternative methods like card payments, wire transfers, etc.

### Terms and Conditions

The terms and conditions should outline important things that your customer should be aware of, like late payment penalties and fees and guarantees or warranties. If you have any other notes, add them here, too.
