Nowadays, there seem to be plenty of ways to make extra money online. 

This can be a side hustle in addition to your core offer or service, a part-time endeavor alongside your regular 9 to 5, or something you develop from scratch and becomes your main business activity.

How you make extra money online will depend on your skill set, talents, and offer—and whether there is a market for them.

With this in mind, here are 4 money-making ideas to consider:

## **1. Create an Online Course**

One effective way to productize your offer is to package up your skills and teach others via an online course.

For example, if you are an artist, could you create a course teaching students the basics? There is no need to stop there; if it's successful, you could then create a more advanced course, and so on. The great thing about online courses is that there are usually no limits to the number of people who can join—unless you stipulate it—and they are a great way of generating a passive income while promoting your [personal brand.](https://invoiceninja.com/5-ideas-to-help-you-stand-out-boost-your-brand-and-keep-clients-happy/)

Your online course could also be a lead magnet to attract your ideal clients to hire you – so it's a win-win. Your personal involvement is up to you; for example, in addition to the course material, you could run weekly live sessions – or simply record a video course and let it run as an evergreen product.

You can sell your course on course platforms such as Udemy, hosted course platforms such as Thinkific and Teachable, or host it yourself.

If you are not a tech whiz, you can always [hire someone](https://invoiceninja.com/4-benefits-of-hiring-a-va-as-a-freelancer/) to help create and manage it.

## **2. Offer Subscriptions and Paid Memberships**

## **3. Join Affiliate Marketing/Referral Programs**

If you use a product you love or know of one that would be helpful for your audience, consider signing up for affiliate or referral programs. Although both involve you promoting another company's products and getting compensated for doing so, there are slight differences between the two. In short, affiliate programs pay a commission for promoting services or products, while referral programs reward existing customers for referring new business to them.

Referral programs use the existing user base to spread the word (i.e. customers refer others), whereas affiliate programs are open to a broader audience and are typically promoted by bloggers, influencers, content creators, and professional affiliate marketers.

## **4. Consider a Paid Email Newsletter**

If you are giving away free, helpful, and valuable content in your email newsletter, consider introducing a paid tier for subscribers who want to access more exclusive information, special discounts, or gain direct access to you.

Email marketing can be lucrative, and the best thing is that you don't necessarily need a massive audience to start making money—just an engaged audience of ideal clients who appreciate that your offer(s) provide a solution to their problems.
