Most freelance writers have heard of (and probably use) [online writing tool](https://invoiceninja.com/6-useful-online-tools-for-freelancer-writers/) assistants such as Grammarly and Hemingway, not to mention file-sharing apps like Dropbox and Google Docs.

However, if you are [starting out on your freelance journey](https://invoiceninja.com/5-things-to-attract-your-first-freelance-clients/), you might not know about some of the other online tools that could be used to provide extra help and support for your freelance writing business and clients.

Here are 5 more useful tools for freelance writers and content creators.

## **1. Social media scheduling tools**

If you post [social media content](https://invoiceninja.com/generate-freelance-leads-from-your-content/) for your brand or your clients, a social media scheduler tool to help you plan content in advance is helpful.

Online schedulers automate all your social media content, giving you more time to focus on your business.

Most schedulers can also provide helpful analytics so you can see how well your social media campaigns are doing. Examples of schedulers include [Hootsuite](https://www.hootsuite.com/), [Vista Social](https://vistasocial.com/) and [Buffer](https://buffer.com/).

To help create and plan timely posts for key dates and events throughout the year that are relatable to your brand, a quick search online for ‘key social media dates’ can help you make the most of these dates, special awareness days, and events.

## **2. SEO research tools**

If you are an [SEO](https://invoiceninja.com/10-essential-seo-factors-entrepreneurs-need-for-a-successful-website/) copywriter, you’ll likely need an online SEO tool for keyword research.

These can vary in price, and some do have a limited free version or free trial period.

In addition to the big names such as [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/), [Moz](https://moz.com/), and [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/), it’s worth shopping around as more products and apps are appearing in the market to help with keyword research and SEO without the hefty price tag.

Other options include [Ubersuggest](https://neilpatel.com/ubersuggest/), a free keyword research tool, and [Keywords Everywhere](https://keywordseverywhere.com/), a freemium Chrome extension.

## **3. Wireframe tools**

While generally used by designers to create a mockup of a website or design, wireframe tools can also be useful for website copywriters when working in conjunction with website designers. They can also show the client where the copy and content might be best placed to allow for the most conversions.

Examples include tools such as [Whimsical](https://whimsical.com/), [Figma](https://www.figma.com/), [Balsamiq](https://balsamiq.com/), and [Lucidchart](https://www.lucidchart.com/pages/).

So, if your work includes UX copy or website copy, wireframe tools can guide your clients as to where the copy should be best placed and how it will look when ‘live’.

## **5. Video makers**

While a video-making tool might not be the first thing that comes to mind when thinking about tools for freelance writers, in today’s fast-paced digital world, visually appealing video content is becoming ever more popular.

You don’t have to be a YouTube sensation or even appear in your videos, but tools like [Lumen5](https://lumen5.com/) can make your content stand out – it can even be used to re-purpose existing [blog posts](https://invoiceninja.com/blog/) into social videos that you can share.

Other similar video-making tools include [Camtasia](https://www.techsmith.com/camtasia/), [InVideo](https://invideo.io/studio), and [Filmora](https://filmora.wondershare.com/). There are lots of video-making tools on the market now – so it pays to shop around and choose the best one for your needs and budget.
