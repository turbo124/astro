From arranging the perfect location to tackling last-minute equipment challenges, filmmakers deal with a lot in every project—even when they meticulously plan ahead. Sometimes, it’s the weather that doesn’t cooperate, while at other times the film team is uncooperative, or the scenes you shot just aren’t coming together the way you imagined.

Given the chaotic nature of filming projects, it's not easy for filmmakers to carve out time to deal with administrative tasks, particularly when it comes to drafting invoices. With looming deadlines and deliverables to juggle, invoicing is also not the best use of their time and energy. If ignored, though, there is a greater likelihood that a disputed invoice could result in financial loss.

This is why we recommend using an invoice template designed for the filming industry.

## What Is a Film Invoice Template?

By definition, a film invoice template is a predesigned document that outlines the costs associated with a filmmaking project. The term 'template' indicates the document is fillable or ready to use.

A film invoice usually provides a breakdown of the expenses relevant to a filmmaking project, including things like crew salaries, equipment costs, and location fees. This invoice is issued by video or film production companies to clients, although some people refer to it as a [video production invoice.](https://invoiceninja.com/invoice-templates-3/video-production-invoice-template/)

## Types of Film Invoices

Filmmaking is a broad term that encompasses various jobs, duties, and services. Whether you're a freelance filmmaker or a video production company, it's a good idea to familiarize yourself with the types of video production invoices that are available and figure out what you can bill your client for.

Generally, film invoices are issued according to time invested or the job performed. Here are the types of film invoices based on time:

- Standard invoice: This is billed at the end of the project and includes all services, goods, additional costs, and taxes.

- Recurring invoice: This is billed weekly or biweekly for an ongoing project (such as weekly vlogs).

- Hourly invoice: Billed each day (by hours), this type of film invoice is usually used for post-video production tasks, such as video and audio editing.

- Prepayment invoice: This invoice is usually sent to the client in parts. Half of the payment is received prior to the start of the project and the other half is received after completion. It usually comes in handy for large projects, such as those that require expensive venue bookings.

- Mixed invoice: This type of invoice is a combination of a standard invoice with any other type mentioned above. For example, you may charge for filming an event and also include your charges for editing the filmed video.

You can also send your client a debit invoice if you wish to charge an extra fee (such as late payment). On the flip side, you can send a credit memo to your client if they overpay you. You can add this credit to their account and deduct the overpaid amount from the next invoice.

## Key Components of a Film Invoice Template

Every invoice template should include the following components:

- Title or name of the project

- Short description of the project

- Unique invoice number

- Client's details (company name, address, and contact)

- Your details (company name, address, and contact)

- Invoice issue date

- Due date

- List of services provided along with quantity and rate of each service 

- Payment terms and conditions (including late fees)

- Payment methods

- Discount information

- Applicable taxes

You can add more items to the invoice if needed, but including all the components mentioned above is a must.
