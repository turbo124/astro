## The Invoicing Process

The way you send bills to clients usually depends on your firm’s specific billing policy. Here is a general step-by-step look into how invoicing works in the legal field:

### Establish Your Billing Terms

Once you get a client and take on a matter, you must establish the billing process. Do this by discussing rates, billing methods, and billable activities. Make sure to document these in writing so that the terms are clear on both sides.

### Log Billable Time and Expenses

Once the work itself has started, it’s crucial to accurately and consistently log all billable activities. This is especially important if you’re billing hours.

### Draft the Bill

Once the project is over (or you’re at the end of the month if it’s a long-term matter), draft all the logged billable expenses into a draft bill.

### Review the Invoice

The draft bill must be sent to the accounting team or senior lawyers who will review the draft to check for corrections. At this point, the bill must be compared against the agreed-upon terms to make sure that everything checks out.

### Send the Bill

After a final bill is drawn, you can send it to your clients, along with a reminder of payment terms and instructions.

### Accept or Follow-up Payments

Clients must pay based on the agreement from the first step and by the invoice’s due date. You can accept payments depending on the methods available to your firm. If the client isn’t holding up their end of the bargain, you may send up follow-up notices.
