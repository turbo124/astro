## Features of a Home Repair Contractor Invoice

When creating a home repair services invoice, here are some elements you should include:

- Invoice number: A unique code used to identify each invoice (e.g., MP-1, 120223, JAN2301, etc.)

- Issue date: ** **Date the invoice was generated and sent to the client

- Due date: Date after which the client will have to pay a late fee

- Company details: Your company logo, name, and address (if you're an independent contractor, you can use your name, home address, or PO Box number)

- Customer details: Client's name and address

- Description of services: Itemization of the home repair services provided, such as bathroom tile repair, foundation repair, or roof repair

- Materials and supplies: As a home repair contractor, you have to purchase materials for your client, which are then installed in their home. Keep in mind that this does not include the equipment required to complete a job. For example, if you're repairing a damaged section of drywall, you'll purchase drywall tape, joint compound, and drywall. You'll also need equipment such as a saw, drill, and drywall knife to get the job done. However, you can only charge your client for the material you purchased for their home, not the equipment you use.

- Labor costs: Your hourly rate, the number of hours worked, overtime pay, and additional expenses such as hazard pay

- Amount due: Total amount due, including any service charges, state or federal taxes, and overtime fees

- Payment terms: Description of how to make payments and what happens in the event a dispute arises

- Payment Information: Your preferred payment methods, such as credit card, check, or wire transfer, along with your ABA routing number and other details

## Frequently Asked Questions

If you’re just getting started in your business, here are answers to some questions you may have before you start billing clients.

### What Tax Is Applicable to Home Repair Services?

As a home repair contractor, you have to pay tax on the materials you purchase. You should also charge your client sales tax. Check your state's rules and regulations on taxation for service-based businesses or contractors.

### How Should Contractors Bill?

Contractors should bill by milestones. This reduces the risk of misunderstandings about the amounts owed for services rendered. However, you may be able to get some or all of your payment upfront if it's a small project (e.g., below $200). Other billing options include weekly, bi-weekly, and monthly payments.
